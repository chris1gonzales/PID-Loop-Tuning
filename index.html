Observed behavior on site

These are field starting points, not final settings — derived from typical HVAC loop characteristics, not a specific manufacturer's engineering manual. Always verify against the actual sequence of operations and equipment response, and confirm the loop is stable (setpoint held, no hunting) before leaving site.

Distech Controls (Niagara)

Proportional Band (%) Integral Time (min)

Native units are Proportional Band and Integral Time in minutes. Counterintuitive but important: a larger PB number means a slower, gentler response, and a larger Integral Time means slower correction of steady-state error — the opposite of what techs used to gain-based systems often assume.

Tridium Niagara (native PID object)

Proportional Gain Integral Constant (repeats/min)

Native N4 PID objects are commonly configured with Proportional Gain directly, plus an integral constant expressed as repeats per minute (reset rate) — typically under 1, with around 0.5 a common starting point. Convert to/from Distech-style band with PB = 100 / Gain.

Johnson Controls Metasys

Proportional Band (%) Integral Time (min) PRAC self-tuning

Native attributes are Proportional Band and Integral Time. Metasys also runs PRAC (Pattern Recognition Adaptive Control) by default, which continuously self-tunes PB/IT — many Metasys loops are never manually tuned at all, so check whether PRAC is enabled before assuming a fixed setting is in effect.

Honeywell — Spyder / CentraLine (legacy)

Proportional Band (%) Integral Action Time (sec)

Native units are Proportional Band and Integral Action Time in seconds. Practical floor to know: integral action effectively disables itself if Integral Time is set below roughly 15 seconds.

Honeywell Optimizer (Advanced / VAV / Unitary)

Proportional Gain Reset rate (repeats/min) Niagara-based

The newer BACnet Optimizer line runs on the Niagara framework, so it follows Tridium-style Proportional Gain and integral reset rate — not the legacy Spyder/CentraLine Proportional Band / Integral Time behavior above. Don't assume the 15-second integral floor applies here.

Core conversions

Proportional Band (%) = 100 / Proportional Gain Proportional Gain = 100 / Proportional Band (%) Reset rate (repeats/min) = 1 / Integral Time (min)

Use these to translate a starting point from one platform's units into another's when cross-referencing panels from different manufacturers on the same job.
